# Competing hypotheses and ranked tests

## Ranking

The ranking is by current evidential support and value as a next investigation, not by how large an effect can be produced.

| Rank | Hypothesis | Present status | Distinguishing prediction | Decisive test and falsification rule |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spatial descriptors improve out-of-batch prediction of superconducting response beyond mean reduction proxy and established batch covariates.** | Important, currently untested in nickelates. | Adding mapped within-film proxy variance/correlation or domain statistics improves prediction on entirely held-out independent growth batches after precursor cation composition, phase fraction, and strain are included. | Preregistered blocked, multi-batch validation. Falsified for the prespecified effect if the out-of-batch improvement is below the minimum relevant gain, reverses unpredictably across batches, or is not reproducible under independent spatial measurement. A predictive gain supports a predictor, not causation or percolation. |
| 2 | **Within the stated Gaussian-threshold model, correlation changes four-neighbour spanning probability at nearly fixed active fraction.** | Supported in exploratory computation; not a reproduction of the manuscript's code or raw data. | At matched mean, marginal active fraction and nearest-neighbour correlation, spanning probability varies systematically with correlation and persists across covariance families, independent seeds, and increasing lattice size. | Repeat with a preregistered factorial sweep, whole-stream independent seeds, finite-size scaling, and exact control of active fraction. Weak/ambiguous if effect is confined to one covariance family or finite size. Falsified in the specified parameter region if model-uncertainty-aware differences are consistently below the prespecified meaningful effect. |
| 3 | **The apparent large change is materially determined by covariance tail shape and/or adjacency convention, rather than by a universal scalar correlation length.** | Supported as a model-uncertainty warning: the present test shows large sensitivity to 4- versus 8-neighbour rules; it samples only two covariance families. | Equal nearest-neighbour correlation does not produce identical cluster geometry when longer-range covariance differs; diagonal adjacency changes spanning rates substantially. | Expand covariance families and compare full empirical covariance functions, not only nearest-neighbour correlation. Falsify the “materially determines” claim only if matched long-range covariance and independently justified adjacency yield stable outcomes across families. |
| 4 | **Mean reduction proxy alone is sufficient to predict a batch's superconducting response once composition, phase and strain are known.** | Plausible competitor, untested. | In independent batches, spatial statistics add no robust predictive value over mean proxy and prespecified covariates. | Compare nested, preregistered models with batch-level holdout and calibrated measurement error. Supported only if the upper uncertainty bound on incremental predictive gain remains below a prespecified practical margin; a nonsignificant coefficient alone is not evidence of sufficiency. |
| 5 | **A favorable-site spanning cluster is itself the superconducting current path in nickelate films.** | Not established and not represented by the binary geometry code. | Mapped active domains should form paths whose connectivity predicts both zero-resistance transport and independent diamagnetic screening, including response to pattern disruption or controlled perturbation. | Requires validated local superconducting-state maps, phase/Josephson coupling or conductance network, transport and magnetic measurements. A geometric map without those measurements cannot support the hypothesis. Failure of connected regions to align with transport/magnetic response would contradict the simple identification. |
| 6 | **The one-orbital slab's fitted density profile is an electrostatic screening length in a nickelate interface.** | Contradicted by the code's structure and non-identifiable fit. | A self-consistent potential response should yield a thickness-converged length robust to fit window, ensemble, and material parameter uncertainty. | Add Coulomb feedback, boundary conditions and material-fitted parameters; compare signed profiles with alternatives. The uploaded calculation cannot test this: it imposes the potential, conserves charge by fixed-density construction, and its representative log-linear fit has poor fit quality. |
| 7 | **Changing interface hopping alone is negligible for nickelate interface physics.** | Not supported as a general statement. | In a symmetry-broken, doped, multiorbital, or interacting model, the response may differ from the near-zero half-filled nearest-neighbour toy response. | Break the relevant particle-hole symmetry in controlled increments, compare fixed density and fixed chemical potential, then add multiorbital/electrostatic physics only with defensible parameters. If effects remain below a predeclared tolerance throughout a validated parameter envelope, only then support a bounded “small effect” claim. |

## Primary recommendation

Prioritize the rank-1 experimental/predictive question, but first complete the computational robustness program in rank 2 and rank 3. The publishable contribution cannot be generic “percolation can create a spanning path”: spatially inhomogeneous local superconductivity and percolative emergence already have substantial precedent in cuprates and disordered superconductors [15] [16] [17]. A defensible nickelate contribution would be that independently measured spatial heterogeneity improves genuinely out-of-batch prediction beyond mean reduction state, precursor composition, structure, and strain. No such material-specific result is demonstrated by the supplied calculation.

## Uncertainty classes to keep separate

For the computed spanning estimate, Wilson intervals quantify only Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on independent realizations and the selected generator. Finite-grid and solver/statistical errors are numerical uncertainty. The choice of spectrum, correlation function, field normalization, adjacency, local-window rule, and lattice size creates model uncertainty. The values of local proxy and spatial length create parameter uncertainty. Spatial-map resolution, proxy calibration, and transport/magnetization sensitivity create experimental uncertainty. The mapping from a dimensionless threshold field to oxygen chemistry or local superconductivity is a separate material-transfer uncertainty and is presently uncalibrated.
